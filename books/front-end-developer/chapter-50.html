Chapter 50 — Capstone Finish, Portfolio and Next Steps

Section 1 — What finished means

This section covers what finished means. A finished capstone is one where every promised feature works end to end: tasks and notes can be added, changed, filtered, searched, and reloaded without a broken step. Finished does not mean perfect; it means complete, tested, and demonstrable.

Thinking about what finished means in the MyDay Dashboard helps to keep the work concrete, because every decision can be tested against a real panel, a real list, or a real button.

Beginners often treat what finished means as a finishing touch, but in this dashboard it shapes how the user finds, changes, and trusts their own tasks and notes.

Work through what finished means in small steps: make one change, open the page, try the feature with the keyboard and the mouse, and only then move on.

A useful habit is to ask what could go wrong with what finished means for a first-time visitor, a hurried visitor, and a visitor on a small phone screen.

When what finished means feels confusing, simplify the surface first and keep the underlying idea the same, because complexity in the interface rarely helps the user.

Good front-end work connects what finished means to the rest of the project, so a change here should not silently break a filter, a count, or a saved list elsewhere.

Here is a short example from the MyDay Dashboard:

  <a href="#">View live demo</a>
</section>

A portfolio entry needs a name, one plain sentence about the job it does, and a link, before any detail about tools or techniques.

A common mistake with what finished means is to rush it, add it everywhere at once, and then struggle to find which change broke the page. Work on one panel at a time, keep a working version saved, and test the dashboard after each step, including reloading the page to confirm that saved tasks and notes are still there. If something looks wrong, undo the last change and re-read the code around it before trying a different fix, because a calm, narrow check usually finds the problem faster than rewriting a whole file.

To check your own work on what finished means, open the dashboard, use the feature as a visitor would, and look for three things: does it do the job, does it still work after a reload, and does it behave sensibly at a narrow width and with the keyboard alone. Write down anything that fails, fix one item at a time, and re-test the whole flow after each fix, because a fix in one panel can affect a count, a filter, or a saved list in another.

Portfolio reviewers spend little time on each project, so the first screen and the first paragraph must explain the job clearly. Honest descriptions build more trust than grand claims: say what you built, what you chose, and what you would improve next.
