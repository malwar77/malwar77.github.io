Chapter 26: Comments, Whitespace and Readable Markup

Section 1: Why Comments Help Future Readers

When authors talk about why comments help future readers, they are really talking about how readable markup serves the reader. A page may look fine on screen while remaining hard to read in its source, and that hidden difficulty shows up later as slow edits and avoidable mistakes. This section treats why comments help future readers as a habit you can practise deliberately. You will learn what good practice looks like, why it helps, and how to apply it without turning every small page into a long chore. The goal is steady, readable work that anyone on a team can follow.

At its heart, why comments help future readers is about making intention visible. Markup already carries meaning through elements and attributes, but layout in the source adds a second layer of meaning for human readers. That layer matters when you return to a page weeks later, or when a colleague needs to fix a problem quickly. By treating why comments help future readers as part of the work, rather than an afterthought, you protect the meaning of the page and make it easier to keep that meaning correct as the page changes.

A frequent pitfall with why comments help future readers is overdoing it. Too many notes, too much blank space, or overly elaborate formatting can make a page harder, not easier, to read. Notes should explain decisions that are not obvious, not repeat the content beside them. Spacing should separate meaningful groups, not scatter a page into fragments. A short note at the right place can save a future editor from undoing a careful decision they do not understand. Consistency matters more than cleverness, because a predictable page is easier to change than a surprising one. When formatting is steady across a page, mistakes stand out sooner and fixes take less time to make.
