Chapter 5: Images and Alt Text

Section 1: Why Images Need Thoughtful Markup

An image can explain, illustrate, or decorate, and the markup you write should reflect which of those jobs the image is doing. Alt text is not a caption and it is not a file name, and its job is to convey the purpose of the image to someone who cannot see it. A missing image still needs a text alternative, because readers on slow connections and readers using assistive technology depend on that text. Choosing a file format and a sensible file size is part of caring for readers, since large files slow a page for everyone. In this section we look closely at why images need thoughtful markup, and we do so in small steps so that nothing important is rushed or left vague.

Beginners often meet why images need thoughtful markup early, yet its full value only becomes clear when you see it working inside a real page. The main idea behind why images need thoughtful markup is simple to state, even though using it well takes a little practice and careful checking. When you understand why images need thoughtful markup, you can read other pages with more confidence and you can explain your own choices more clearly. A good way to learn why images need thoughtful markup is to build one tiny example, open it in a browser, and then change one thing at a time. Small changes teach more than large ones, because you can see exactly what each change does to the page you are viewing.

Assistive technology also depends on clear structure, so a careful choice here helps people who do not read the page visually. The browser does a great deal of work behind the scenes, and your job is to give it clear instructions it can follow reliably.

Clear instructions matter because browsers try to be helpful when markup is unclear, and that helpful repair can hide a mistake.
