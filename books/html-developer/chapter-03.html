Chapter 3: Headings, Paragraphs and Text Structure

Section 1: Why Document Structure Matters

Remember that HTML is a language for documents, and its strength is in describing meaning clearly rather than controlling every visual detail. That separation is helpful, because it lets the same document work on many devices, in many browsers, and for many kinds of readers. As you practise why document structure matters, aim for pages that are simple, honest, and complete, rather than pages that try to impress with complexity. Simple pages load faster, break less often, and are easier for other people to read, maintain, and build upon in the future. End each practice session by reviewing one thing you learned and one thing you still want to test, because that review turns practice into progress.

The chapter theme of Headings, Paragraphs and Text Structure runs through this section, and keeping that theme in mind helps you see how each detail fits the larger whole. Return to earlier sections when a new idea reminds you of an old one, since revisiting strengthens memory more than reading once and moving on. Finally, trust the process of learning by doing, and let each small page you build add one more solid piece to your growing understanding. An experienced author checks spelling, structure, and links with the same care, because readers notice carelessness even when they cannot name it. When you share a page, you are sharing your thinking, so clear markup is also a form of clear communication with your future self and others.
