Section 1: What a Loop Does

What a Loop Does is the focus of this section, and it belongs to the wider topic of loops and control flow in Go. Go keeps its looping tools deliberately simple: there is only the for statement, and it covers every kind of repetition. A for loop can look like a classic counting loop, like a while-style loop that runs while a condition holds, or like an infinite loop that you leave with break. One keyword, several shapes, and the shape you choose should make the loop's stopping rule obvious to the next person who reads it.

A while-style for loop drops the init and post parts and keeps only the condition, running while the condition stays true. This shape suits work where you do not know in advance how many rounds you need, such as reading input until it passes a check. An infinite loop drops the condition as well, and it must contain a break or a return somewhere, or it will truly never stop. Used sparingly, both make loop logic clearer rather than more tangled.
