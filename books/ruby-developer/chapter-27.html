Section 1: What a Regular Expression Is

This section is about what a regular expression is. At first the name may sound technical, but the idea underneath it is ordinary and practical, and you have probably already met it in everyday life without calling it by this name. A regular expression is a small pattern that describes a shape of text, rather than one exact piece of text. Instead of saying find the word cat, you can say find any three letter word, or any word that starts with a letter and ends with a number. Ruby lets you write that pattern between slashes and use it to test, find, replace or split text. Once you see a pattern as a description, the strange symbols start to make sense as short instructions.

Patterns become powerful because real text is messy. Names have different lengths, phone numbers have spaces or dashes, and user input rarely arrives in perfect form. A pattern lets you check the shape without listing every possible value. The trick is to keep the pattern as simple as the job needs. A short, clear pattern that checks the important shape is better than a long, clever pattern that nobody, including future you, can read or fix.

Every symbol in a pattern has a job, and most jobs are simple on their own. A dot stands for any one character, a digit symbol stands for any digit, and square brackets list the characters you will accept. Repetition symbols say how many times the previous part may appear. Anchors say where in the string the match must start or end. When a pattern confuses you, read it one piece at a time from left to right and say each piece out loud in plain words.
