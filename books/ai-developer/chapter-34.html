To understand what a task helper does, start from the problem it solves rather than from the code. Ask yourself what a person would do by hand, slowly, with a pencil and a single page. Then ask which part of that hand work is repetitive, easy to describe, and safe to automate. The parts that are repetitive are good candidates for code. A good local tool does not pretend to know more than it does. It does one job, it shows what it did, and it leaves the final decision with the person using it. That honesty is not a weakness. Go back, simplify the step, or split it into two smaller steps until the hand version feels obvious. Only then should you turn the steps into Python.
