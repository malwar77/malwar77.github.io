The key is to see the pattern behind the example. Once you notice the pattern, you can change one small part of it and predict what will change in the result. The example is deliberately tiny, and that is a strength. A tiny example lets you focus on one idea without juggling five other ideas at the same time. Small changes are your best teacher, because when something breaks you know exactly which change caused it. A useful habit is to keep your experiments small and your notes close. If an idea confuses you, write down in your own words what you expected to happen and what actually happened. They do not pretend to understand everything at once. They break a puzzle into smaller pieces until one piece becomes clear, and then they build back up from there.
