Examples that actually work are worth more than perfect descriptions, because readers copy examples first and read theory later. Documenting errors honestly saves readers hours, because they learn what went wrong and how to fix it instead of guessing. Authentication belongs near the front of your docs, since nobody can try anything until they know how to prove who they are. Required fields and optional fields must be labelled plainly, or readers will send incomplete requests and blame the API. A contract is an agreement about shape: which fields exist, what types they hold, and which ones a client may rely on. When code and contract disagree, clients break, so tests that compare the two protect everyone who depends on your API.
