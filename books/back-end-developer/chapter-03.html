Idempotent is a long word for a simple idea, repeating the action gives the same result. PUT is usually idempotent, while POST is not, because repeating POST may create duplicates.

That difference matters when a network retry sends the same request twice. Design your endpoints so a retry does not accidentally create two orders or two accounts. A clear method choice also helps caches, because caches know GET answers can be reused. Do not hide a delete action behind a GET request, even if it feels quicker to build. Shortcuts like that cause real harm when a crawler or a preview tool follows the link.

Status codes also guide search engines, browsers and other programs that use your service. Returning 200 for every answer, even errors, confuses those programs and hides problems. Be honest with status codes, and your service becomes easier to debug and to trust. Headers can also control caching, language, encoding and how a body should be read. Small details in headers often explain why a page looks right in one place and wrong in another.

When you are stuck, compare the request headers with the response headers, line by line. That comparison often reveals a missing type, a wrong length, or an unexpected redirect.
